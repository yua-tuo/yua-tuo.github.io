Working Papers

Optimal Treatment Under Spillover Effects and Noncompliance

(Job Market Paper)

Abstract. This paper considers optimal treatment allocation in the presence of spillover effects in both compliance and outcomes. We show that an individual's outcome depends on neighbors' compliance types besides her own type. For optimal allocation, we either maximize welfare subject to a budget constraint or minimize cost subject to a welfare goal, based on the characteristics of the target population. We propose an optimal randomized treatment rule and show that it achieves uniform asymptotic welfare efficiency with an n−1/2 convergence rate and is uniformly asymptotically feasible. We apply the proposed rule to an anti-conflict experiment in public middle schools in New Jersey and show that not incorporating both layers of spillovers can lead to misallocation of treatment.

Causal Inference in RCTs with Multiple Arms

Abstract. When there is more than one treatment group in an RCT with noncompliance, local average treatment effects (LATE) are not directly comparable because each treatment has different compliers. We provide a partial-identification method to compare two treatments in the presence of noncompliance. In particular, we derive bounds on the average difference between pairs of treatment effects for those who would comply with both treatments, as well as bounds for other subpopulations. We also apply our bounds to an experiment evaluating the impacts of different depression treatments on economic behavior in India.
